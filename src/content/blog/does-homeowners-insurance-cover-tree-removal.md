When a tree comes down, the first question is usually who pays. The short answer is that homeowners insurance helps in some situations and not in others, and the line between them is about what caused the tree to fall and what it landed on.

> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Coverage usually applies when a covered event, such as wind, hail, lightning, or fire, brings a tree down onto a covered structure.
> - Debris removal is often capped per tree and per incident, and the limits vary by policy.
> - Removing a healthy tree before it causes damage is generally not covered.
> - Vehicles are usually handled by auto insurance, not your homeowners policy.

## When Does Homeowners Insurance Cover a Fallen Tree?

Insurers generally pay when a covered event, such as wind, hail, lightning, or fire, knocks a tree onto a covered structure like the house, a garage, a fence, or a shed. The policy then pays toward repairing the damage and, often, toward getting the tree off the structure.

Because the cause matters, take photos of the tree and the damage before anything is moved, and write down what happened and when.

## What About Removing the Tree Itself?

Debris removal is often limited. Many policies cap what they will pay per tree and per incident, and those caps differ from one policy to the next. We are not going to guess at your limits. Your declarations page, or a call to your insurer or agent, will tell you the real numbers.

## When Is Tree Removal Not Covered?

Preventive removal is generally not covered. If a healthy tree is simply in the way, or you would like it gone before it causes a problem, that cost usually falls on you. A tree that is already dead or decaying is a gray area, so ask your insurer before you assume either way.

Vehicles are the other common surprise. A tree that lands on a car is typically an auto insurance claim rather than a homeowners one.

## What Should I Do Before Calling a Tree Service?

1. Make the area safe and stay away from anything touching a power line. Our guide on [trees on power lines](/blog/tree-on-a-power-line-what-to-do) explains why.
2. Photograph the damage and the tree from several angles.
3. Call your insurer or agent and ask how your policy handles tree removal and debris.
4. Ask the tree service for a written estimate you can send along with your claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my insurer pay to remove a tree that has not fallen?

Generally not. Preventive removal of a healthy tree is typically not covered. If you think a tree is a risk, it is worth having it assessed and asking your insurer about your situation.

### Does insurance pay if the tree fell on my car?

Usually that is handled by your auto insurance rather than your homeowners policy. Check with both if you are unsure.
