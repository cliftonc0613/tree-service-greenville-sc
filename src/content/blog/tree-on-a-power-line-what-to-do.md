After a storm, a tree that is touching or tangled in a power line looks like a cleanup job. It is not. It is a safety emergency, and the first people to call are the utility and, if anyone is in danger, 911, not a tree crew.

> **Key Takeaways**
>
> - Stay at least 30 feet back from a downed line, and treat every downed line as live, even if it is not sparking.
> - Report the hazard to your utility or to emergency services.
> - If a line is on your vehicle, stay inside and call 911 unless there is immediate danger such as fire.
> - Do not trim or remove trees within 10 feet of overhead lines. Only line-clearance-qualified crews should work closer.

## What Should I Do If a Power Line Is Down?

Stay at least 30 feet back, and keep everyone else back too. Treat every downed line as live. A line does not have to be sparking to be dangerous.

Then report it to the utility, or to emergency services if anyone is hurt or in danger. Do not try to move the line, the branches touching it, or the tree.

## What If a Line Is on My Car?

Stay inside the vehicle and call 911, unless there is immediate danger such as a fire.

## Who Do I Call?

Call the utility named on your bill. Duke Energy serves most of the Greenville area, but the bill is the quickest way to be sure of who serves your address. We are leaving phone numbers off this page on purpose, because numbers change; your bill and the utility's website have the current one.

## Can I Cut a Tree Away From a Power Line Myself?

No. The utility's guidance is to never trim or remove trees within 10 feet of overhead lines, and to call before working near them. Only line-clearance-qualified crews should work closer than that, which is utility-qualified work and not a homeowner's job or an ordinary tree crew's. If a tree you want trimmed is close to the lines, call the utility first and ask how they handle it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a downed line safe if it is not sparking?

No. Treat every downed line as live, even if it is not sparking.

### Can I move branches that are only touching a power line?

No. Do not touch the tree or the branches. Call the utility and wait for them to clear it.

### Does the tree service handle the power company?

No, and neither should you. The utility handles the lines. A tree service handles the tree once the area is safe.
