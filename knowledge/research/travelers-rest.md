## Travelers Rest

**Location**
- City in Greenville County, about 10 miles north of Greenville; the northernmost city in the county. 2020 Census population 7,788. Source: [Wikipedia, Travelers Rest, SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelers_Rest,_South_Carolina) (via WebSearch).
- US 25 passes along the east side of the city heading north toward the Blue Ridge and Hendersonville, NC. US 276 runs from downtown Greenville and becomes Travelers Rest's Main Street. Sources: Wikipedia, above; [AARoads US 25](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-025-sc) (via WebSearch).
- The Prisma Health Swamp Rabbit Trail runs between Greenville and Travelers Rest; its northern end is in Travelers Rest (TrailLink lists Tate Rd south of White Horse Rd). Sources: [TrailLink](https://www.traillink.com/trail/prisma-health-swamp-rabbit-trail); [VisitGreenvilleSC](https://www.visitgreenvillesc.com/things-to-do/outdoor-activities/swamp-rabbit-trail/) (via WebSearch).

### History of Travelers Rest (added 2026-11-16)
- Name: from its days as a stopover for travelers. One account says William Bishop ran an inn where drovers slept indoors while their animals were penned outside; another says it was a stopping point for cattle drovers from Kentucky, North Carolina, and Tennessee. A Greenville County Historical Society paper (scanned, 1960s era) says stagecoaches on the Asheville to Augusta or Columbia route also paused here. Sources: [Travelers Rest, "But What About that Name?"](https://travelersrestsc.com/?p=6761); [Greenville County Historical Society paper](https://d8e7jbdw4fu0e.cloudfront.net/17224/be86ffa0-2e13-11f0-8afd-392fc7de7a74-vUXupf8.full.pdf); [SC Encyclopedia](https://scencyclopedia.org/sce/?p=11459) (via WebSearch). The copy says only "a stopover for travelers and cattle drovers".
- Spelling: "Travellers Rest" until the mid-1940s, when postmaster William F. Griffin officially changed it (same historical society paper). Single document; confirm with the Travelers Rest Historical Society.
- Not used: the 1808 founding date (single source) and any incorporation date (not found). A Toccoa, Georgia "Travelers Rest" inn appeared in results and is a different place.

### History Museum of Travelers Rest (added 2026-11-16)
- 1926 building, built at 8 S. Main Street by W.H. Vest as a grocery store. Donated to the Travelers Rest Historical Society in 2010 on condition it be moved; it now stands at 3 Edwards Street on land donated by the Bob Roe family, faced with bricks from the former Travelers Rest High School (built 1956, razed 2011). Sources: [Upcountry SC listing](https://upcountrysc.com/listing-details/travelers-rest/history-museum-of-travelers-rest/61075555/); [Travelers Rest, museum page](https://travelersrestsc.com/?p=797) (via WebSearch).

### Trees, storms, and soil (added 2026-11-16)
Retrieved with WebSearch (Firecrawl out of credits). None of these sources is specific to Travelers Rest; they cover Clemson and Upstate or Piedmont conditions, and the copy says so.
- Storm failures: Clemson says even Category 1 hurricane winds can topple healthy trees when soils are saturated, crowns are heavy, or limbs are already compromised. Dense crowns catch wind. Properly pruned trees survive hurricanes better than unpruned or improperly pruned trees. Yard trees that often fail in SC include water oak, pecan, Bradford pear, maple, and pine; live oak, bald cypress, southern magnolia, river birch, and sabal palmetto are more resilient. Source: [Clemson HGIC, Managing Trees for a Hurricane Resistant Landscape](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/managing-trees-for-a-hurricane-resistant-landscape/) (via WebSearch).
- Saturated ground: water acts like a lubricant, so roots slip more easily. In the Piedmont there is rock 40 to 60 inches down, and in places shallower, that roots cannot penetrate. Sources: [SC Public Radio, The dangers of saturated ground for trees](https://www.southcarolinapublicradio.org/show/making-it-grow/2022-09-15/the-dangers-of-saturated-ground-for-trees); [SC Public Radio, Shallow roots and deep issues](https://www.southcarolinapublicradio.org/sc-news/2024-01-16/shallow-roots-and-deep-issues-with-water-and-soil-in-south-carolina) (via WebSearch). Not tied to red clay or to Travelers Rest, so the copy says "much of the Piedmont".
- Pine beetles: beetles tend to attack pines stressed by drought, flooding, crowding, or storm damage. Signs: pitch tubes on the trunk, reddish-brown or changing needle color, exit holes. Sources: [Clemson HGIC, Common Pine Bark Beetles in Urban Settings](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/common-pine-bark-beetles-in-urban-settings-identification-and-treatment-of-these-species/); Alabama Extension summary of stress factors (via WebSearch). The drought-stress detail in SC reports is from 2017 and earlier.
- Common species: loblolly pine is one of the most common pines in SC; southern red oak is common in the Piedmont; tulip poplar has thin bark that is easily injured and wood that rots quickly. Sources: [Clemson Forestry blog](https://blogs.clemson.edu/fnr/?p=139); [Clemson HGIC, Oak](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/oak/); a Clemson-linked tree selection guide (via WebSearch). Statewide and Piedmont sources, so copy says "common in this part of the Upstate".
- Not used: Hurricane Helene tree damage in Travelers Rest (no source found; the Swamp Rabbit Trail reopened Oct. 3, 2024 and later cleanup was reported, but nothing for Travelers Rest), red clay and windthrow (no source ties them), and any current Swamp Rabbit Trail status.

### Travelers Rest tree rules and program (added 2026-11-16)
Town-specific sources. Retrieved with WebSearch and WebFetch (Firecrawl out of credits).
- City code Chapter 3.08, "Protection of Trees, Shrubs or Plants on or Affecting Public Property" (code supplement 19). Source: [Travelers Rest Municipal Code, Ch. 3.08](https://travelersrestsc.com/municipal_codes/supplement-19/HTML/level2/TIT3PUWO_CH3.08PRTRSHPLAFPUPR.html) (via WebFetch).
  - 3.08.040 covers street, park, and other public trees: species, spacing, and planting rules near utility lines (small trees only, 30 feet maximum, under or within 10 lateral feet of overhead wires). Topping any public tree is unlawful; the public works director may exempt storm-damaged trees or trees under wires where other pruning is impractical.
  - 3.08.050: the public works director may inspect, and order removal of, a tree on private property that is a public nuisance: infectious disease or insect problems, dead or dying, obstructing street lights, traffic signs, or free passage of pedestrians or vehicles, or a threat to safety. Owners get 15 days after notice. Failure to comply can bring a municipal court citation (up to $500 or 30 days).
  - 3.08.060: if a nuisance is not abated, the city may do the work and the cost becomes a lien on the property.
  - No section in this chapter sets a permit requirement for planting, pruning, or removing trees, and the chapter does not regulate removal of healthy private trees. Other chapters (zoning, development) were not checked, so the copy says "this chapter" and tells readers to check with the city.
- Tree City USA: a city news post dated April 6, 2021 says Travelers Rest "has been awarded the designation of Tree City USA". Communities must reapply each year; current status not confirmed. Source: [City of Travelers Rest, Tree City USA Status](https://travelersrestsc.com/?p=4372) (via WebFetch). Copy says "named a Tree City USA in 2021".
- Not used: yard waste pickup schedule (city page appears to date from about January 2024; Greater Greenville Sanitation, weekly), Trailblazer Park "2% canopy" (low-reliability database), Athens Park shade trees (minor), Helene debris programs (ended).
