## Grind depth, roots, and chips
- Typical depth: about 4 to 6 inches below grade for new grass, about 8 to 12 inches for replanting a tree; patios or buildings need deeper work. Most grinders reach 13 to 18 inches; going past 12 is rarely practical. Sources: contractor pages (Lawn Phix, Ironwood Earthcare, Elite Tree Service, others) and [TCI Magazine, Operating the Stump Grinder](https://tcimag.tcia.org/safety/operating-the-stump-grinder/) (via WebSearch). Mostly contractor marketing, so treated as typical ranges, not standards.
- Grinding removes the stump and nearby surface roots, not the whole root system. Remaining roots decay over years, and the ground can settle. Same sources.
- Chips usually fill the hole; options are keeping as mulch, mixing into soil, or hauling. Fresh chips can temporarily tie up nitrogen (one source only; copy says "can"). The spot may settle and need topping up.

## Buried utilities and SC811
- South Carolina requires anyone excavating, even on small home projects, to notify SC811 before digging. Standard notice is three full business days, not counting the request day; emergencies are handled 24/7. Sources: [SC811 state law](https://sc811.com/state-law/); [SC811 Excavator Manual 2026](https://sc811.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Excavator-Manual-2026.pdf) (via WebSearch).
- **Not confirmed:** whether stump grinding counts as "excavation" under the law. No source addresses it. Copy does not claim it is required; it tells readers to ask SC811.
- The Underground Facility Damage Prevention Act was amended effective May 22, 2026, and the SC811 state-law page still showed older text. Do not state notice periods more precisely than "standard notice is three full business days" without checking SC811.
