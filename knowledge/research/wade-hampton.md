## Wade Hampton

**Location**
- Census-designated place (unincorporated) in central Greenville County, named for Civil War general and South Carolina governor Wade Hampton. The city of Greenville is on its southwest edge and Taylors is to the northeast. 2020 population 21,482 (Wikipedia). Source: [Wikipedia, Wade Hampton, SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Hampton,_South_Carolina) (via WebSearch).
- Wade Hampton Boulevard (US 29) is a commercial arterial running northeast through the Wade Hampton and Taylors areas. Sources: [Wikipedia, US 29 in SC](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_29_(South_Carolina)); [AARoads US 29](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-029-sc) (via WebSearch, see taylors.md).

### Landmarks on Wade Hampton Boulevard (added 2026-11-16)
- Wade Hampton High School, 100 Pine Knoll Drive, one block off Wade Hampton Blvd and Pleasantburg Drive; opened 1960; primarily serves Wade Hampton and Taylors. Sources: [Wikipedia, Wade Hampton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Hampton_High_School); [Greenville County Schools profile](https://www.greenville.k12.sc.us/Schools/profile/whhs) (via WebSearch).
- McBride's Office Supply, 832 Wade Hampton Blvd: one-story building by the Greenville firm Townes and Murphy, Modern style; National Register listed September 16, 2024 (NRIS 100010864). Source: [SC Dept. of Archives and History record](https://schpr.sc.gov/index.php/Detail/objects/S10817723097); [NRHP additional documentation](https://historypointer.com/nris/resource/100010864) (via WebSearch).
- Not used: Camp Sevier (WWI training camp, roughly 2,000 acres from Wade Hampton Blvd to State Park Road) and Piedmont Park Baptist Church, which rest on a single school PDF. No roadside marker found on the boulevard.

Source: [SC Dept. of Archives and History](https://schpr.sc.gov/index.php/Detail/objects/S10817723097); a trade-publication summary (via WebSearch). The Upstate Business Journal article named in the paste was not found. Used: card text updated.

  - Hampton Monument (13355 E Wade Hampton Blvd): in Greer, Spartanburg County, a 1933 marker erected by the Stonewall Chapter of the United Daughters of the Confederacy that honors three Hamptons. The "1776 Hampton Massacre" detail in the paste was not confirmed.
