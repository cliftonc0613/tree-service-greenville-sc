## Wade Hampton

**Location**
- Census-designated place (unincorporated) in central Greenville County, named for Civil War general and South Carolina governor Wade Hampton. The city of Greenville is on its southwest edge and Taylors is to the northeast. 2020 population 21,482 (Wikipedia). Source: [Wikipedia, Wade Hampton, SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Hampton,_South_Carolina) (via WebSearch).
- Wade Hampton Boulevard (US 29) is a commercial arterial running northeast through the Wade Hampton and Taylors areas. Sources: [Wikipedia, US 29 in SC](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_29_(South_Carolina)); [AARoads US 29](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/us-029-sc) (via WebSearch, see taylors.md).

### Landmarks on Wade Hampton Boulevard (added 2026-11-16)
- Wade Hampton High School, 100 Pine Knoll Drive, one block off Wade Hampton Blvd and Pleasantburg Drive; opened 1960; primarily serves Wade Hampton and Taylors. Sources: [Wikipedia, Wade Hampton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Hampton_High_School); [Greenville County Schools profile](https://www.greenville.k12.sc.us/Schools/profile/whhs) (via WebSearch).
- McBride's Office Supply, 832 Wade Hampton Blvd: one-story building by the Greenville firm Townes and Murphy, Modern style; National Register listed September 16, 2024 (NRIS 100010864). Source: [SC Dept. of Archives and History record](https://schpr.sc.gov/index.php/Detail/objects/S10817723097); [NRHP additional documentation](https://historypointer.com/nris/resource/100010864) (via WebSearch).
- Not used: Camp Sevier (WWI training camp, roughly 2,000 acres from Wade Hampton Blvd to State Park Road) and Piedmont Park Baptist Church, which rest on a single school PDF. No roadside marker found on the boulevard.

### Review of the owner's pasted landmark list (2026-11-16)
- McBride's Office Supply (832 Wade Hampton Blvd): 1962 Modernist commercial building with Googie influences, by Townes and Murphy; National Register listed September 16, 2024; redeveloped as Prosper, a 7,000-square-foot office project by Franklin Real Estate Development. Source: [SC Dept. of Archives and History](https://schpr.sc.gov/index.php/Detail/objects/S10817723097); a trade-publication summary (via WebSearch). The Upstate Business Journal article named in the paste was not found. Used: card text updated.
- Not added (outside the Wade Hampton community, or unconfirmed):
  - Richland Cemetery (N Church St and E Stone Ave), Hampton-Pinckney Historic District, and Pettigru Historic District: all in the city of Greenville, not on Wade Hampton Boulevard (Hampton-Pinckney is named for Gov. Wade Hampton III but is not on the boulevard).
  - Hampton Monument (13355 E Wade Hampton Blvd): in Greer, Spartanburg County, a 1933 marker erected by the Stonewall Chapter of the United Daughters of the Confederacy that honors three Hamptons. The "1776 Hampton Massacre" detail in the paste was not confirmed. Sources: [HMDB](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=10400); [Historical Marker Project](https://historicalmarkerproject.com/markers/HM6P6_hampton_Greer-SC.html).
  - Museum and Gallery at Bob Jones University (1700 Wade Hampton Blvd): the on-campus museum is closed to the public while the museum pursues a new location, so it is not a place a visitor can go. Source: [Museum and Gallery, M&G closure](https://museumandgallery.org/mg-closure/).

### Trees, neighborhoods, and rules (added 2026-11-16)
Retrieved with WebSearch and WebFetch (Firecrawl out of credits).
- Neighborhoods: real-estate listing pages describe Wade Hampton Terrace (mid-century brick ranches, lots from 0.3 to 1.3 acres, mature landscaping with azaleas and shade trees) and Wade Hampton Gardens (all-brick ranches on lots from 0.30 to 1.0 acres, established trees). Sources: [Palmetto Park, Wade Hampton Terrace](https://www.palmettopark.com/greenville/wade-hampton-terrace); [Palmetto Park, Wade Hampton Gardens](https://ppk26.palmettopark.com/greenville/wade-hampton-gardens) (via WebSearch). Marketing pages, so the copy says "listings describe". The named neighborhoods use a Greenville address, so they may be inside city limits; the copy says "near Wade Hampton Boulevard" and does not claim they are in the community.
- Rules: the community borders the city of Greenville, so some addresses fall inside city limits and others are in unincorporated Greenville County. The county's tree preservation article, in a 2023 public review draft, applies to new development needing a grading permit (see service-tree-removal.md). The City of Greenville's current rules for existing single-family lots were not confirmed (one city engagement page said they have no protections for trees on existing lots with homes and the ordinance was being revised). The copy tells readers to check with the city or county and states no permit rule.
- County canopy: Greenville County has a tree canopy study with the SC Forestry Commission and the Green Infrastructure Center. Search summaries give a countywide figure of 38% and a possible goal of 46%, but the county PDF is too large to read with the available tool and the news URL returned 404, so **the numbers are unverified and not used.** Wade Hampton and Taylors are not named in anything retrieved. The copy does not mention the study.
- Not used: Pleasant Valley Park or any county park near Wade Hampton (not confirmed to exist or be nearby); Helene debris (ended); Hampton-Pinckney, Gower Estates, and other city neighborhoods.
