## Warning signs that a tree needs a closer look
- Sudden lean (a slow, long-standing lean is less urgent; not every lean is dangerous); cracks separating the main trunk (urgent); soil cracking or lifting at the base of a leaning tree; mushrooms or fungus at the base (often rotten roots); large hollows or decayed, peeling areas; dead or rotten branches (often the branches, not the whole tree, need to come out); forks with two similar-sized stems that can split. Sources: ISA "Recognizing Tree Risk" ([Flint copy](https://cityofflint.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/TreesAreGood_Recognizing-Tree-Risk_0721.pdf)); [UF/IFAS Tree Risk Assessment](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/?p=6257) (via WebSearch). Commercial tree-service blogs in the results were not used.

## Methods
- Sectional removal with ropes, whole-tree felling where there is room, and crane lifts are standard trade methods. This is general practice, not a sourced local fact, and the existing copy already describes the crane option.

## Rules that may apply
- Greenville County's AnswerBook page (trees) covers only trees within county right-of-way: Public Works will trim and remove those. It does not address private-lot trees. Source: [Greenville County AnswerBook, trees](https://greenvillecounty.org/AnswerBook/trees.aspx) (via WebSearch).
- The county's tree preservation article, in a 2023 public review draft of the Unified Development Ordinance, applies to new development in unincorporated areas needing a grading permit. **Not confirmed against the adopted ordinance.** Source: [Greenville County tree preservation draft](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/planning/pdf/07.Tree%20Preservation.Public%20Review%20Draft%2008-11-23.pdf) (via WebSearch).
- City of Greenville: a city engagement page says the current ordinance has no protections for trees on existing single-family lots, but the city was revising it. **Not confirmed; not used in copy.** Source: [City of Greenville public input](https://publicinput.com/K1373) (via WebSearch).
- A commercial tree-service blog says "generally no permit" for a tree on your own property. Not used (commercial source, contradicted by another page from the same company).
- Copy says rules differ by city and county, tells readers to check first, and names no permit requirement either way.

## Power lines
- Duke Energy says never trim or remove trees within 10 feet of overhead lines; only line-clearance-qualified crews may work closer; call before working near lines. Sources: Duke Energy Trees and Safety and Trees FAQ; SC Office of Regulatory Staff (see service-tree-trimming.md).

## Bradford pears
- Clemson's Bradford Pear Bounty lets a property owner swap up to five Bradford pears for free native trees. The owner registers in person with a photo of the cut-down tree. Event dates in the results were from 2021, so **check Clemson Extension for current dates.** Source: [Clemson News, Bradford Pear Bounty](https://news.clemson.edu/clemson-brings-battle-against-invasive-bradford-pear-trees-to-states-capital/) (via WebSearch).
