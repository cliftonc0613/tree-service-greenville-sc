## Five Forks

**Location**
- Census-designated place (unincorporated) in eastern Greenville County, about 11 miles east-southeast of downtown Greenville. 2020 Census population 17,737. Source: [Wikipedia, Five Forks, SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Forks,_South_Carolina) (via WebSearch).
- Simpsonville is about 6.1 miles south and Mauldin about 4.5 miles southwest of Five Forks, so Five Forks is north of Simpsonville and northeast of Mauldin. Source: [Roadside Thoughts, Five Forks](https://roadsidethoughts.com/sc/five-forks-xx-greenville-census.htm) (via WebSearch). Secondary source; directions agree with Wikipedia's "eastern Greenville County".
- The name comes from the meeting point of five major roads. Woodruff Road is an east/west corridor and the area's commercial hub. Source: [Greenville County, Five Forks Area Plan](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/apps/LongRangePlanning/uploads/FiveForksAreaPlanOrdinance5314.pdf) (via WebSearch).

**Note:** The Simpsonville page does not say "Five Forks on its north side". That wording implies the two border each other, which no source states. "North of Simpsonville" on this page is supported by the distance listing.

### Trees, growth, storms, and county rules (added 2026-11-16)
Retrieved with WebSearch and WebFetch (Firecrawl out of credits).
- Growth: Five Forks was very rural into the early 1990s, then grew rapidly into a shopping and housing area. Population 8,064 (2000), 14,140 (2010), 17,737 (2020). Source: [Wikipedia, Five Forks, SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Forks,_South_Carolina) (via WebFetch; census figures, check against Census before quoting numbers). Copy uses only "rural into the early 1990s, then grew quickly".
- Gilder Creek forms the southern boundary of Five Forks and is a tributary of the Enoree River. Source: Wikipedia (via WebFetch). A county area plan draft also lists Gilder Creek among local creeks (via WebSearch).
- The five roads, per The Post and Courier as cited by Wikipedia: Woodruff Road (SC 146), Batesville Road, Scuffletown Road, Bennetts Bridge Road (SC 296), Five Forks Road (SC 296). Not used in copy (secondary citation only).
- Tornado: on April 14, 2019, an EF1 tornado with peak winds of 100 mph ran from 2 miles southwest of Simpsonville to 3 miles southeast of Five Forks. Source: [NWS Greenville-Spartanburg public information statement](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/api/1/nwstext/201904160147-KGSP-NOUS42-PNSGSP); [NWS Greenville County tornadoes](https://www.weather.gov/gsp/GreenvilleCountyTornadoes); [City of Simpsonville](https://www.simpsonville.com/administration/page/city-officials-crews-work-clear-tornado-damage) (via WebSearch).
- Five Forks Area Plan: written with a committee of residents starting in 2018; it addresses heavy traffic, loss of trees, and inconsistent growth, and proposes a Five Forks Environmental Preservation Overlay District to preserve open space and trees. Sources: Wikipedia (via WebFetch); [county plan PDF](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/apps/LongRangePlanning/uploads/FiveForksAreaPlanOrdinance5314.pdf) (via WebSearch summary; the PDF was too large to fetch); [July 2021 council summary CP-2021-01](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/Council/_Agenda/Meetings%20of%202021/County%20Council/2021.07.20/Summary%20CP-2021-01.pdf). Adoption: Wikipedia says council approved it in August 2021; not confirmed from county minutes, so the copy does not claim adoption. Overlay standards (for example a 10-inch tree rule seen in a draft) were not confirmed and are not used.
- County tree preservation rules, as drafted (Aug 2023 public review draft), apply to new development that needs a grading permit. Same source as the Berea research.
