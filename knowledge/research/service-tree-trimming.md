## Pruning standards
- Cuts go just outside the branch collar, and the collar stays intact. Flush cuts leave dead tissue above and below the cut. To reduce height or spread, cut back to a side (lateral) branch at least one-third the diameter of the limb being removed. Sources: ISA "Pruning Mature Trees" (Seattle and Abbotsford copies), [Seattle Trees guide](https://seattle.gov/documents/Departments/Trees/PlantingAndCare/Pruning/Pruning_MatureTrees.pdf) (via WebSearch).
- Topping: ISA calls it perhaps the most harmful pruning practice known. It can strip half or more of the leafy crown, expose remaining limbs to sun, and the tree usually needs pruning again within a few years. Source: ISA "Why Topping Hurts Trees" ([Seattle copy](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/trees/plantingandcare/pruning/whytoppinghurts.pdf)) (via WebSearch).
- Crown removal: "no more than about 25% of the crown at once, and less for mature trees" is a general ceiling from ISA-derived guides, not a fixed rule. The exact ISA wording was not returned. **Confirm against the current ISA publication before launch.** Copy says "about a quarter" and "a ceiling, not a target".

## Crape myrtles (Clemson)
- Clemson HGIC calls topping crape myrtles unnecessary and says it leads to unattractive, unhealthy plants. The only pruning needed is structural, only when necessary. If pruning is needed to stimulate growth, late winter or early spring. Choosing a variety whose mature size fits the space can avoid pruning. Sources: [Clemson HGIC, Crape Myrtle Pruning](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/crape-myrtle-pruning/); [HGIC, February is for pruning](https://hgic.clemson.edu/forget-the-hearts-and-chocolates-february-is-for-pruning/) (via WebSearch).

## Bradford pears (South Carolina)
- State approval to end new nursery sales of Bradford pears (Pyrus calleryana) starting October 1, 2024. Owning one stays legal. Source: [Clemson News, Bradford pear to be banned for sale in SC](https://news.clemson.edu/?p=194130) (via WebSearch). The sources describe the ban as upcoming, so **confirm it took effect as planned.** Copy says "approved ending new nursery sales starting October 1, 2024".

## Power lines
- Duke Energy: never trim or remove trees within 10 feet of overhead lines; only line-clearance-qualified contractors may work closer. Call Duke before pruning near lines. The property owner is responsible for keeping the service line (pole to meter) clear and should ask Duke to de-energize it before tree work. Sources: [Duke Energy, Trees and Safety](https://duke-energy.com/community/trees-and-rights-of-way/how-we-manage-trees/trees-and-safety); [Duke Energy, Trees FAQ](https://duke-energy.com/community/trees-and-rights-of-way/faq); [SC Office of Regulatory Staff, tree pruning and power line safety](https://ors.sc.gov/consumers/electric-natural-gas/tree-pruning-and-power-line-safety) (via WebSearch). The 10 feet is a safety clearance for lines up to 50 kV, not a legal threshold.
