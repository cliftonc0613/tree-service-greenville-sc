## The pitch in one paragraph

This rerun changes the call from the November 9 playbook, and the reason is better data. The first run had no localized Google results and no search volume. This one pulled a live Greenville-located Google results page and real keyword numbers through DataForSEO. The map pack is still locked by two heavyweights (Greenville Tree Co at 459 reviews and Integrity Tree Co at 353), but organic page one is soft. Below the pack sit a WordPress lead-gen style site, BBB, a dead 411 Locals site, a county government page, a cost aggregator, three Reddit threads, two inner pages and a pile of aggregators. Demand is real at roughly 2,000 searches a month across the city phrases, and close to 2,900 once the suburbs within 25 miles are counted (Simpsonville, Taylors, Easley and Greer lead). The catch: a new site can only fight for organic spots under a map pack it will never enter, and other rank-and-renters are working the same phrases. Build it lean, rank organically, and rent it to one of the 20+ hungry independents who don't own the pack.

**Owner-reachability.** High. Tree service is owner-run, and owners are named on their own sites. There are 20+ independents in the county who don't own the pack, so the renter pool is deep.

The softer risk is market awareness: at least one Greenville County company (McClaran's Tree Care on upstate-tree-experts.pages.dev) appears to already rent a lead-gen site, so some owners know the model.

If it stalls on page two at day 90, fold it into a Greenville County area site or move the template to Anderson (210 searches, smaller field).

## Renting the leads

**The pitch.** Call the owner of an independent outside the pack. Mckenzie Tree (91 reviews), Arbor Care Solutions, Finley, Daisy and Southeastern are the first five. Say the site is already getting calls for tree work in Greenville and offer a free seven-day trial with calls forwarded to them. After the week, call back and ask how many jobs they closed. If the trial worked, pitch the monthly number and take a card on file that day. Play back recorded calls if they hesitate.

**Pricing.** Tree jobs run $500 to $3,000+, so one closed removal a week covers the rent. At one to three calls a day, charge a flat $1,200 to $2,000 a month. Below that, early on, pitch $75 to $125 per qualified call, or about 10% of closed jobs, then convert to flat once volume holds. Revenue share stays a later option and needs read access to their books.

**Trust check.** Watch how fast and how well they answer during the trial. Run one secret-shopper call before they start paying. Storm-week response is the real test: a renter who lets calls ring out after a storm is the wrong renter.

## Running the portfolio

Once rented, watch call volume in GHL, not rankings. Spot four or five organic still pays. Investigate only if calls drop off hard. Google core updates target big affiliate and content sites, not a ten-page local tree site.

Saturation isn't the risk at the portfolio level, but it is real on this keyword set, which is why this build stays lean and why the 90-day checkpoint matters. If Greenville works, the same template goes to Anderson and Spartanburg (210 searches each).

Two lessons from this rerun worth keeping. Always use a localized SERP before calling competition, since national web search overstated the templated sites here. And a dead site still ranking on page one is one of the best green signals there is.
