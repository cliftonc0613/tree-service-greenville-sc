## Permits and stormwater (Greenville County)
- Stormwater requirements apply to unincorporated, non-SCDOT land development disturbing one acre or more, or 10,000 sq ft or more for water quality. They also reach participating municipalities (Simpsonville, Mauldin, Travelers Rest, Fountain Inn). Source: [Greenville County Stormwater Design Manual, Ch. 2](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/LandDevelopment/pdf/designmanual/DesignManualChapter2revJan2018.pdf) (via WebSearch).
- Single residential lots disturbing one acre or more, not part of a larger plan, need a Land Disturbance (Grading) Permit. Sites between 5,000 sq ft and one acre need a simplified stormwater and sediment plan, and the building permit can stand in for a separate grading permit. Source: [Greenville County, Erosion and Sediment Control Standard](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/LandDevelopment/pdf/ErosionSedimentContolStandard.pdf) (via WebSearch).
- Not confirmed: current fees, and whether thresholds changed in the 2025 stormwater ordinance update ([2025 ordinance](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/Council/_Agenda/Meetings%20of%202025/County%20Council/2025.06.17/Stormwater%20Ordinance%20FINAL.pdf)). Copy tells readers to confirm thresholds with the county.

## Open burning
- State law requires notice to the SC Forestry Commission before burning certain vegetative material; land-clearing burns have extra conditions under Regulation 61-62.2, and local governments can be stricter. Sources: [SC Regulation 61-62.2](https://www.des.sc.gov/sites/des/files/Library/Regulations/R.61-62.2.pdf); [Greenville County, Open Burning](https://www.greenvillecounty.org/AnswerBook/BurningReg.aspx) (via WebSearch). Older sources still say DHEC; regulation text appears in several versions. Copy stays general and does not state distances, pile sizes, or phone numbers.

## Forestry mulching vs traditional clearing
- Mulching uses one machine to cut and grind vegetation into mulch left on the ground. Traditional clearing uses several machines to push, pile, grub stumps, and haul debris. Mulching generally leaves stumps and roots, which matters under a slab or foundation. Mulching disturbs soil less. Sources: [Virginia Tech Extension, 465-341](https://pubs.ext.vt.edu/465/465-341.html); [Wikipedia, Mulcher](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Mulcher); contractor blogs (via WebSearch). Statistics like "90% of roots retained" come from one commercial blog and are not used.
